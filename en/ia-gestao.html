Two fronts

A model is only worth it if it reaches production and proves impact.

I work at both ends because each limits the other. Without technical rigor, the team delivers models nobody can defend. Without management, good models stay prototypes or solve problems the business doesn't have.

Pillar 01

AI and Machine Learning

From credit risk to generative AI, always asking which decision the model changes.

  • Propensity and NBA/NBO. Propensity models by product category, first at Carrefour and today at Smiles. I created the NBA (Next Best Action), which decides which action to take with each customer. The next step is the NBO (Next Best Offer), which also chooses which offer to deliver.
  • Causal inference. Propensity tells you who tends to buy, not who buys because of the campaign. That's why I use control groups and holdouts to measure incrementality.
  • Risk and classification. A Random Forest behavior score on Hadoop and the PD (probability of default) model for vehicle financing, at Itaú. At Santander, classification of NPS detractors and their causes.
  • NLP and similarity. Satisfaction drivers extracted from customers' free text, and a product similarity matrix with Nutri-Score integrated.
  • Generative AI. Multi-agent systems with LangGraph and MCP, conversational chats to extract insights from data, such as SmilesGPT, and applications that support agile development of digital solutions.
Pillar 02

Managing data and teams

The Data & Analytics function at Smiles: 14 people, 2 direct coordinators and three business areas served.

  • Data platform. Modernization and unification of the platform, with a medallion architecture (Bronze, Silver and Gold), Databricks and governance with Unity Catalog.
  • Roadmap and stakeholders. I align priorities with CRM, Vitrines and Product and tie every analytics deliverable to a business goal.
  • Analytical maturity. Replacing “how many campaigns did we run” with “how much did each campaign add”. That requires consistent measurement and governance of the control groups.
  • Team. Organized by fronts, with direct coordination and a data-driven culture built on collaboration and impact.
  • Leadership path. Data Scientist and Consultant, then Data Science Manager at Carrefour, Data & BI Manager at Banco Carrefour and, today, Head at Smiles.

Where the two fronts meet

Governance of control groups. Measuring incrementality is a statistical problem: the control group must stay untouched and comparable to the treated group. But it is only solved through a management decision: who owns the control group, who may contact it and for how long. I proposed that the D&A team take over this management for the propensity models at Smiles, a condition for evolving from NBA to NBO with gains that can be proven.
