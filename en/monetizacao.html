Data monetization

DataBank and the Gutenberg algorithm.

Every retailer keeps years of purchase history, but usually treats it as a by-product of operations. DataBank, which I co-founded, started from the opposite idea: treat that history as an asset and build on it a product able to generate revenue and to prove that this revenue is new. In 2022 the startup was recognized at the Innovation Challenge of Tech for Retail, in Paris.

How the product works

Purchase dataCustomers' purchase history as the raw material.
Propensity modelGutenberg estimates who is most likely to buy each product or category.
SegmentationAudiences ranked by propensity, ready for the advertiser or for CRM.
ActivationCampaigns on owned CRM channels, with Media Trading and CRM in the same flow.
MeasurementResults compared with a control group that was not contacted.
incrementality = ȳcontacted − ȳcontrol

Only what exceeds what the control group, with no contact, would have bought anyway counts as platform revenue. That is what separates data monetization from inflated attribution.

Design decisions behind the result

Propensity on first-party history

The model learns from what customers actually bought, without depending on third-party data. The quality of the ranking comes from the depth of the history.

Activation on owned CRM channels

Whoever controls the channel controls who is contacted, when and with which message. That makes the campaign measurable end to end.

A control group that is not contacted

Keeping some customers untouched lets you read the difference in results as the effect of the campaign, and not as a purchase that would have happened anyway.

Who we create value for

For the retailer

A new revenue line from an asset that already exists: customers' own purchase data.

For the industry and the advertiser

Media that reaches the people most likely to buy, with results measured against a control group.

For the end customer

More relevant offers on the channels they already use, instead of mass communication.

What happened next

The platform and the product were absorbed into Carrefour's internal strategy, and DataBank was closed in 2023. From it I took a conviction I apply today at Smiles: data only becomes money when the model, the activation and the measurement of incrementality work together, and measurement is the part that most often gets left out.
